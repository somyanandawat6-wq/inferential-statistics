## What Is Inferential Statistics?

Inferential Statistics uses sample data to draw conclusions about a larger population.

It helps us estimate population parameters, understand uncertainty, and make informed decisions using data.

## Population vs. Sample

- **Population:** The entire group we want to study.
- **Sample:** A subset of the population selected for analysis.

Since studying an entire population is often impractical, we use samples to learn about population characteristics.

## Sampling Methods

### Simple Random Sampling

A sampling method in which population members are randomly selected.

### Stratified Sampling

The population is divided into homogeneous subgroups called strata, and samples are selected from each subgroup.

### Cluster Sampling

The population is divided into naturally occurring groups called clusters, and selected clusters are sampled.

## Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

The Central Limit Theorem explains how the sampling distribution of the sample mean approaches a normal distribution under suitable conditions as the sample size increases.

It is an important foundation for statistical estimation and inference.

## Confidence Intervals

A confidence interval provides a range of plausible values for a population parameter based on sample data.

It helps quantify the uncertainty associated with a sample estimate.

## Standard Error and Margin of Error

- **Standard Error:** Measures the sampling variability of an estimator.
- **Margin of Error:** Represents the amount added to and subtracted from an estimate to form an interval at a chosen confidence level.

## Z-Distribution vs. T-Distribution

- **Z-Distribution:** Used in appropriate inference procedures, including tests or intervals for a mean when the population standard deviation is known.
- **T-Distribution:** Commonly used for inference about a mean when the population standard deviation is unknown and estimated from sample data.

The appropriate method depends on the assumptions and characteristics of the problem.

## Confidence Level and Significance Level

The confidence level is commonly expressed as \(1-\alpha\), where \(\alpha\) is the significance level in the corresponding inferential framework.

For example, a 95% confidence level corresponds to \(\alpha = 0.05\).
